\begin{abstract}
A heteroscedastic probit, a probit whose noise scale depends on features, estimates its scale coefficients poorly from a few hundred target rows.
We centre its ridge penalty at a fit on a large related source sample and pick the strength by grouped cross-validation on a grid that holds both endpoints, target-only and source-only.
Against the plain ridge-at-zero target fit of the same package (\texttt{scaleprobit} \nPkgVersion), on a public football era-shift task with \nFbTestGames{} later test games, log-loss changes by \nFbBDiffTO{} nats per row (SE \nFbBSeTO) at \nFbBGames{} target games and by \nFbFullDiffTO{} (SE \nFbFullSeTO) with the full \nFbFullGames-game season; on a men's-to-women's basketball task (\nHpTestGames{} test games) it ties.
Using the source fit unchanged is as good or better: it beats ours beyond SE on football from \nFbCGames{} games on (by \nFbFullDiffSO, SE \nFbFullSeSO, at the full season) and on basketball at \nHpAGames{} games (by \nHpADiffSO, SE \nHpASeSO), and ties at the other sizes, so the gain over target-only comes from the source fit, not from adapting to the target.
Our pre-registered test (beat target-only, source-only and pooled at once) failed on both datasets.
Differences are paired on the same test rows over \nSubsamples{} target subsamples per size; SEs bootstrap whole test games.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

Many binary outcomes are threshold crossings of a quantity that keeps moving.
A game ends with the home team ahead or not; a temperature ends above a mark or not.
The classic answer is a Brownian closed form \citep{stern1994}: with signed distance $d$ to the threshold, remaining horizon $h$ and base scale $s$ (the typical movement per unit of $\sqrt{h}$), the probability is $\Phi(d / (s\sqrt{h}))$.
Its weak spot is the scale.
How far the quantity can still move depends on the state of the game, so a heteroscedastic probit \citep{harvey1976} learns the scale as a log-linear function of features, as weather post-processing does \citep{gneiting2005,messner2014}.

We hit the limit of this model when the target sample is small.
With a few hundred target rows the scale coefficients are poorly determined, and a plain ridge shrinks them towards zero, which is towards the closed form again.
Often a large related sample exists: earlier seasons, another league, another product.
Fitting on it alone ignores how the target differs, and pooling needs the source rows at fit time.
We ask whether a ridge pulled towards the source fit, instead of towards zero, beats all three.

\section{The change}
\label{sec:change}

We keep the model fixed and change only the penalty.
For row $i$ the fitted scale, the score and the probability are
\begin{equation}
\sigma_i = s_i \exp(a + w^\top z_i), \qquad
\eta_i = \frac{u_i}{\sigma_i} + b, \qquad
P(y_i = 1) = F\bigl(\mathrm{clip}(\eta_i, -c, c)\bigr), \qquad
u_i = \frac{d_i}{\sqrt{h_i}}.
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
With parameters $\theta = (a, w, b)$ set to zero, this is the closed form of Section~\ref{sec:problem}.
The plain variant penalises towards zero, and we penalise towards the source fit $\theta_0$:
\begin{equation}
\hat\theta_{\text{plain}}(\lambda) = \arg\min_\theta\; \ell_T(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\bigl(\lVert w\rVert^2 + b^2\bigr),
\qquad
\hat\theta(\lambda) = \arg\min_\theta\; \ell_T(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert \theta - \theta_0\rVert^2 .
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}

{\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$d_i, h_i, s_i$ & signed distance to the threshold, remaining horizon, base scale \\
$z_i$ & log scale features, standardised with the \emph{source} mean and sd \\
$a, w, b$ & scale intercept, scale slopes, bias of the score \\
$F, c$ & standard normal CDF (or Student-$t$), clip bound $c = \nClip$ \\
$\ell_T$ & summed negative log-likelihood on the target rows \\
$\theta_0$ & the source fit (plain variant with $\lambda = \nLamGridA$ on the source) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\par}
\vspace{2pt}

Three choices make it more than a prior mean.
First, the penalty covers every coordinate, including the scale intercept $a$, which the plain variant leaves free, because we expect the overall level of the scale to be the term a small target estimates worst (the post-hoc ablation in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} gives \nFbFullDiffFreeA{} on football and a tie on basketball).
Second, we freeze the standardisation of $z$ at the source values, so $\theta_0$ means the same thing on both samples.
Third, $\lambda$ comes from grouped cross-validation (folds that keep all rows of a game together) over
$\{0, \nLamGridA, \nLamGridB, \nLamGridC, \nLamGridD, \nLamGridE, \infty\}$ with \nCvFolds{} folds, and ties go to the larger $\lambda$.
Both endpoints are on the grid: $\lambda = \infty$ returns $\theta_0$ exactly (source-only), and $\lambda = 0$ is the unpenalised target fit started at $\theta_0$ (target-only).
The CV table therefore shows directly whether adapting to the target paid.
We fit with L-BFGS-B \citep{byrd1995lbfgsb}, a quasi-Newton solver with box bounds, using an analytic gradient and boxes $|a| \le \nBoundA$, $|w_j|, |b| \le \nBoundW$.
The gradient is zero on clipped rows, where the loss is flat.

None of this is new in general.
A ridge centred at a source fit is MAP adaptation (a prior centred at the old model, \citealp{chelba2006}), biased regularisation \citep{kuzborskij2013} and L2-SP (an L2 penalty towards pretrained weights, \citealp{li2018l2sp}), and \citet{tian2023glmtrans} study GLM transfer with a CV-chosen strength.
Our delta is narrow: the heteroscedastic probit with the three choices above, measured against both endpoints.
Proposition~\ref{prop:slope} restates the classic local argument for this likelihood; the proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

\begin{proposition}[local risk, after \citealp{kuzborskij2013}]
\label{prop:slope}
Take the quadratic approximation of $\ell_T$ around the target optimum $\theta_*$, with total Fisher information (the curvature of the log-likelihood) $J \succ 0$ from $n$ target rows, penalty $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\theta-\theta_0)^\top \Lambda (\theta-\theta_0)$ with $\Lambda$ diagonal, $\Lambda \succeq 0$, $\Lambda \ne 0$, and gap $\delta = \theta_0 - \theta_*$.
The expected excess risk $R(\lambda)$ in the per-row metric $J/n$ has slope $R'(0) = -2\,\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda)/n < 0$.
So some $\lambda > 0$ beats target-only for any finite $\delta$, and for $\Lambda \succ 0$, $R(\lambda) \to \delta^\top (J/n)\, \delta$ as $\lambda \to \infty$.
\end{proposition}

So in the quadratic regime a small pull always lowers the expected risk.
The proposition does not say cross-validation finds the right $\lambda$ on a few dozen groups, and the likelihood is not convex, so we claim no unique optimum.

\textbf{Using it on other data.}
The method needs, per row, a binary outcome $y$, a signed distance $d$ to the threshold (positive when $y = 1$ is ahead), a horizon $h > 0$ left until the outcome is decided, a base scale $s > 0$ (one constant is enough, for instance the source's spread of final changes per $\sqrt{h}$) and positive scale features $x$ (we use $z$ = the standardised $\log x$).
The recipe is three steps: (1) fit the plain variant on the source with $\lambda = \nLamGridA$ and freeze its parameters and standardisation as $\theta_0$; (2) on the target rows, score the grid above by grouped CV, grouping rows that share an outcome; (3) refit the chosen $\lambda$ on all target rows and read the CV table, which says whether target-only, source-only or a point in between won.
In the package this is \texttt{prior = fit\_source(Xs, ys, distance=ds, horizon=hs, base\_scale=ss)} and then \texttt{ScaleProbitTransferCV(prior=prior).fit(Xt, yt, distance=dt, horizon=ht, base\_scale=st, groups=gt)}; \texttt{cv\_results\_} holds the table.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.}
Football (play-by-play from \citealp{nflverse}): source seasons \nFbSrcYears{} (\nFbSrcGames{} games, \nFbSrcRows{} rows), target pool season \nFbPoolYears{} (\nFbFullGames{} games), test seasons \nFbTestYears{} (\nFbTestGames{} games, \nFbTestRows{} rows).
In both sports the horizon $h$ is the seconds of regulation left and the base scale $s$ is one constant, the standard deviation of the source's final margins divided by the square root of a full game's seconds, so the closed form is calibrated on the source alone.
On football the distance $d$ is the home margin plus the pregame point spread times the share of the game left (Stern's drift: the favourite's expected remaining edge counts as distance); the \nFbNFeat{} scale features are the remaining time, the margin, timeouts left and the pregame total-points line.
Basketball \citep{sportsdataverse}: source men's league \nHpSrcYears{} (\nHpSrcGames{} games, \nHpSrcRows{} rows), target pool women's league \nHpPoolYears{} (\nHpFullGames{} games), test \nHpTestYears{} (\nHpTestGames{} games, \nHpTestRows{} rows); $d$ is the home margin and the \nHpNFeat{} scale features are the remaining time, the scoring rate so far and the margin.
Each game gives \nFbRowsPerGame{} random game states, and $y = 1$ when the home team wins.
The test seasons come after every training season, so the football task is an era shift (train on old seasons, test on new ones), and nothing tunes on them.
A synthetic generator with known truth gives a regime map over \nSynCells{} cells (shift $\times$ target rows, \nSynSeeds{} draws each); the shift is the Euclidean length of the difference between the target's and the source's true $\theta$.

\textbf{Arms.}
Closed form ($\theta = 0$, the trivial baseline); target-only (the package's unmodified plain variant at the same version, $\lambda$ by the same CV; this is the upstream the change is measured against); source-only ($\theta_0$ as it is); pooled (source and target rows with a domain indicator in the scale); ours.
Generic learners: logistic regression \citep{pedregosa2011sklearn} target-only and pooled, on $[u, u z, z]$ (the linearised scale model); LightGBM (gradient-boosted trees, \citealp{ke2017lightgbm}) target-only, source-only and fine-tuned from the source booster, on the raw state variables ($d$, $h$, margin, lines, timeouts or scoring rate, $u$).
\nAblations{} pre-registered ablations: $\lambda$ fixed at \nLamGridA, \nLamGridC{} and \nLamGridE, a Student-$t$(\nTdf) link, and target standardisation.
Every arm sees the same target rows and the same source rows where it uses any; each picks its one capacity knob by the same \nCvFolds-fold grouped CV on the target rows ($\lambda$, the logistic penalty, or LightGBM's boosting rounds).

\textbf{Metric and SE.}
The primary metric is held-out log-loss $L = -\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i [y_i \log p_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)]$ in nats per row.
Target sizes are \nFbAGames, \nFbBGames, \nFbCGames, \nFbDGames{} (and \nHpEGames{} for basketball) games, \nSubsamples{} random subsamples each, plus the full pool.
The paired SE of $L_{\text{ours}} - L_{\text{arm}}$ is a block bootstrap over test games (\nBootDraws{} draws), resampling whole games because rows of one game are dependent, plus the between-subsample variance divided by \nSubsamples.
We call a difference inside \nZcrit{} SE a tie.
Before the run we pre-registered the primary test: ours against target-only, source-only and pooled at every subsampled size, with success if, at some subsampled size, all three paired upper \nCiPct\% bounds are below zero on either real dataset.

Which endpoint wins depends on the shift, and on a small target grouped CV does not reliably tell a real shift from noise.
\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The pre-registered success test failed on both real datasets, at every size.
The method improves on the plain target fit on football from \nFbBGames{} games on and in \nSynWinsTO{} of \nSynCells{} synthetic cells, and ties it on basketball; on both real tasks the source fit used as it is had a lower mean log-loss than ours at every size, so the gain over target-only comes from the source fit, not from target adaptation.
We do not claim the method beats source-only or pooling, nor that it generalises beyond these two sports and the synthetic generator.

After the main run we tried \nVariantsTried{} post-hoc variants of the $\lambda$ selection (a \nDenseGrid-point grid, slopes pulled harder, the one-SE rule, repeated CV; Appendix~\ref{app:variants}); none was kept, and the one-SE rule is source-only by another name.
The \nAblations{} ablations in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} were pre-registered; its free-intercept row is post-hoc.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:slope}}
\label{app:proof}

\textbf{Given.}
1) Quadratic approximation: $\ell_T(\theta) \approx \ell_T(\hat\theta) + \tfrac12 (\theta-\hat\theta)^\top J (\theta-\hat\theta)$ with $J \succ 0$ and $\hat\theta$ the unpenalised target fit.
2) $J(\hat\theta - \theta_*) = g$ with $\mathbb{E}[g] = 0$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(g) = J$.
3) Objective $Q_\lambda(\theta) = \ell_T(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\theta-\theta_0)^\top\Lambda(\theta-\theta_0)$, $\Lambda$ diagonal, $\Lambda_{jj} \ge 0$, at least one $\Lambda_{jj} > 0$ (the package uses $\Lambda = I$).
4) Risk $R(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat\theta(\lambda)-\theta_*)^\top (J/n) (\hat\theta(\lambda)-\theta_*)\bigr]$.
5) Matrix rules: $\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}A^{-1} = -A^{-1}\tfrac{dA}{d\lambda}A^{-1}$; $\mathrm{tr}(XY) = \mathrm{tr}(YX)$; $\mathbb{E}[g^\top M g] = \mathrm{tr}(MJ)$ for $\mathbb{E}[g] = 0$, $\mathrm{Cov}(g) = J$.

\textbf{Choose:} $A = J + \lambda\Lambda$ and $m = \lambda A^{-1}\Lambda\delta$.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $\hat\theta(\lambda) - \theta_* = m + A^{-1}g$.
\begin{align*}
0 &= J(\theta-\hat\theta) + \lambda\Lambda(\theta-\theta_0) && \text{gradient of $Q_\lambda$, given 1 and 3}\\
A\theta &= J\hat\theta + \lambda\Lambda\theta_0 && \text{collect the terms in $\theta$}\\
A(\theta-\theta_*) &= J(\hat\theta-\theta_*) + \lambda\Lambda(\theta_0-\theta_*) && \text{subtract $A\theta_* = J\theta_* + \lambda\Lambda\theta_*$}\\
\theta-\theta_* &= A^{-1}g + \lambda A^{-1}\Lambda\delta && \text{given 2, multiply by $A^{-1}$}
\end{align*}
The last line needs $A$ invertible, therefore, for $x \ne 0$:
\begin{align*}
x^\top A x &= x^\top J x + \lambda\, x^\top \Lambda x && \text{definition of $A$}\\
&> 0 && \text{$J \succ 0$, $\lambda \ge 0$, $\Lambda \succeq 0$}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Same method, on the risk.} Substitution in given 4:
\begin{align*}
R(\lambda) &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(m + A^{-1}g)^\top \tfrac{J}{n} (m + A^{-1}g)\bigr] && \text{first part}\\
&= m^\top \tfrac{J}{n} m + 2\, m^\top \tfrac{J}{n} A^{-1}\mathbb{E}[g] + \mathbb{E}\bigl[g^\top A^{-1}\tfrac{J}{n}A^{-1} g\bigr] && \text{expand, $m$ is not random}\\
&= \underbrace{\lambda^2 \delta^\top\Lambda A^{-1}\tfrac{J}{n}A^{-1}\Lambda\delta}_{B(\lambda)} + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{n}\mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}JA^{-1}J)}_{V(\lambda)} && \text{$\mathbb{E}[g] = 0$, given 5}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Need to prove:} $R'(0) = -2\,\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda)/n$.
Choose $\beta(\lambda) = \delta^\top\Lambda A^{-1}\tfrac{J}{n}A^{-1}\Lambda\delta$, so $B(\lambda) = \lambda^2\beta(\lambda)$, with $\beta$ differentiable at $0$ because $A^{-1}$ is (given 5, $A$ invertible above).
\begin{align*}
B'(\lambda) &= 2\lambda\,\beta(\lambda) + \lambda^2\beta'(\lambda) && \text{product rule}\\
B'(0) &= 2\cdot 0\cdot\beta(0) + 0\cdot\beta'(0) = 0 && \text{$\lambda = 0$}
\end{align*}
For the variance:
\begin{align*}
V'(\lambda) &= \tfrac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{tr}(-A^{-1}\Lambda A^{-1} J A^{-1} J) + \mathrm{tr}(A^{-1} J (-A^{-1}\Lambda A^{-1}) J)\bigr] && \text{product rule, given 5}\\
V'(0) &= -\tfrac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda J^{-1}J J^{-1}J) + \mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}J J^{-1}\Lambda J^{-1}J)\bigr] && \text{$A = J$ at $\lambda = 0$}\\
&= -\tfrac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) + \mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda J^{-1}J)\bigr] && \text{$J^{-1}J = I$}\\
&= -\tfrac{2}{n}\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) && \text{cyclic trace, given 5}
\end{align*}
The sign needs $\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) > 0$, therefore:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) &= \textstyle\sum_j (J^{-1})_{jj}\Lambda_{jj} && \text{$\Lambda$ diagonal}\\
&= \textstyle\sum_j (e_j^\top J^{-1} e_j)\,\Lambda_{jj} && \text{unit vectors $e_j$}\\
&> 0 && \text{$J^{-1} \succ 0$, all $\Lambda_{jj} \ge 0$, one $> 0$}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Therefore} $R'(0) = B'(0) + V'(0) = -2\,\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda)/n < 0$, and some $\lambda > 0$ has $R(\lambda) < R(0)$, the target-only risk.

\textbf{Same method, on the limit} ($\Lambda \succ 0$).
\begin{align*}
\lambda A^{-1} &= (J/\lambda + \Lambda)^{-1} \to \Lambda^{-1} && \text{divide $A$ by $\lambda$}\\
m &= \lambda A^{-1}\Lambda\delta \to \delta, \qquad A^{-1} \to 0 && \text{first line}\\
R(\lambda) &\to \delta^\top \tfrac{J}{n}\delta + 0 && \text{substitution in $B$ and $V$}
\end{align*}
That is the source-only risk. \qed

\section{Origin, reproduction and cuts}
\label{app:repro}

\textbf{Origin.} We first built the method for a private application, and this is the one statement we make about it. \privateline

{\raggedright
\textbf{Reproduction.} The package is \texttt{scaleprobit} (Python, SciPy L-BFGS-B, \citealp{virtanen2020scipy}).
Benchmark results are those committed at \texttt{\nResultsCommit} with package version \nPkgVersion; the package is our own, not a vendored library, so there is no upstream diff, and the plain variant in the same package is the unmodified baseline.
For scale only (one run, one multi-core CPU machine, not a speed claim), the benchmark took \nFbWallS{} s (football), \nHpWallS{} s (basketball) and \nSynWallS{} s (synthetic) of wall time.
Commands, from the repository root: \texttt{python -m bench.run\_football}, \texttt{bench.run\_hoops}, \texttt{bench.run\_synthetic}, \texttt{bench.variants.run football|hoops}, \texttt{bench.variants.synthetic\_one\_se}; then, with \texttt{PYTHONPATH=src:.}, \texttt{paper/results/refit\_full.py}, \texttt{paper/results/ablate\_free\_intercept.py}, \texttt{paper/results/make\_numbers.py} and \texttt{paper/figures/make.py} regenerate every number and figure in this note.
The full-season refit reproduces the benchmark log-loss of every scaleprobit arm, and its per-game SE matches the block SE at the full season (\nFbFullSeGameTO{} against \nFbFullSeBlockTO{} on football).
Cuts, none silent: a third real dataset; the R arms \texttt{glmx::hetglm} \citep{zeileis_glmx} and \texttt{glmtrans}; the Platt and grouped-$\lambda$ ablations, and the free-intercept ablation at subsampled sizes (it was run afterwards at the full season only); the football source starts later than planned to bound download and fit time.
Basketball target files carry no usable pregame spread, so neither side uses Stern's drift there.
LightGBM uses one fixed parameter set; only its rounds are chosen by grouped CV.
