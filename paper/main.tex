\begin{abstract}
A heteroscedastic probit model, a probit whose noise scale depends on features, cannot identify its scale coefficients from a few hundred target rows.
We centre its ridge penalty at a fit on a large related source sample and choose the penalty strength by grouped cross-validation over a grid that holds both endpoints, the target-only fit and the source-only fit.
On a public football era-shift task the source-centred ridge beats the plain target-only fit by \nFbBDiffTO{} nats of log-loss per row (SE \nFbBSeTO) at \nFbBGames{} target games and by \nFbFullDiffTO{} (SE \nFbFullSeTO) with the full \nFbFullGames-game season, and it ties target-only on a men's-to-women's basketball task.
It does not beat the source-only endpoint: on football source-only is better by \nFbFullDiffSO{} (SE \nFbFullSeSO) at the full season, on basketball by \nHpADiffSO{} (SE \nHpASeSO) at \nHpAGames{} games, so the pre-registered test (beat target-only, source-only and pooled at once) failed on both real datasets.
Every comparison is paired on later held-out seasons, averaged over \nSubsamples{} target subsamples per size, with a block bootstrap over test games.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem}
\label{sec:problem}

Many binary outcomes are threshold crossings of a quantity that keeps moving: a game ends with the home team ahead or not, a level ends above a mark or not.
The classic model for this is a Brownian closed form \citep{stern1994}: with signed distance $d$ to the threshold, remaining horizon $h$ and a base scale $s$, the probability is $\Phi(d / (s\sqrt{h}))$.
The weak spot is the scale.
How much the quantity can still move depends on the state, so a heteroscedastic probit, a probit with a feature-dependent scale \citep{harvey1976}, learns the scale as a log-linear function of features, as in ensemble post-processing \citep{gneiting2005,messner2014}.

With a few hundred target rows the scale coefficients are not identified.
A plain ridge shrinks them towards zero, which means towards the closed form.
Often a large related sample exists: earlier seasons, another league, another product.
Fitting on it alone ignores how the target differs, and pooling both needs the source rows at fit time.
The question is whether a ridge pulled towards the source fit, instead of towards zero, does better than all three.

\section{The change}
\label{sec:change}

The model is fixed; only the penalty changes.
For row $i$ the fitted scale, the score and the probability are
\begin{equation}
\sigma_i = s_i \exp(a + w^\top z_i), \qquad
\eta_i = \frac{u_i}{\sigma_i} + b, \qquad
P(y_i = 1) = F\bigl(\mathrm{clip}(\eta_i, -c, c)\bigr), \qquad
u_i = \frac{d_i}{\sqrt{h_i}}.
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
The parameters are $\theta = (a, w, b)$.
At $\theta = 0$ the model is the closed form above.
The plain variant penalises towards zero; ours penalises towards the source fit $\theta_0$:
\begin{equation}
\hat\theta_{\text{plain}}(\lambda) = \arg\min_\theta\; \ell_T(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\bigl(\lVert w\rVert^2 + b^2\bigr),
\qquad
\hat\theta(\lambda) = \arg\min_\theta\; \ell_T(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\lVert \theta - \theta_0\rVert^2 .
\label{eq:ridge}
\end{equation}

\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule

$z_i$ & log scale features, standardised with the \emph{source} mean and sd \\
$a, w, b$ & scale intercept, scale slopes, bias of the score \\
$F, c$ & standard normal CDF (or Student-$t$), clip bound $c = \nClip$ \\
$\ell_T$ & summed negative log-likelihood on the target rows \\
$\theta_0$ & the source fit (plain variant with $\lambda = \nLamGridA$ on the source) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three choices make it more than a prior mean.
First, the penalty covers every coordinate, including the scale intercept $a$, which the plain variant leaves free.
Second, the standardisation of $z$ is frozen at the source values, so $\theta_0$ means the same thing on both samples.
Third, $\lambda$ comes from grouped cross-validation (folds that keep all rows of a game together) over
$\{0, \nLamGridA, \nLamGridB, \nLamGridC, \nLamGridD, \nLamGridE, \infty\}$ with \nCvFolds{} folds, and ties go to the larger $\lambda$.
Both endpoints are on the grid: $\lambda = \infty$ returns $\theta_0$ exactly (source-only), and $\lambda = 0$ is the unpenalised target fit started at $\theta_0$ (target-only).
The CV table therefore shows directly whether adapting to the target paid.
The fit is L-BFGS-B \citep{byrd1995lbfgsb} with an analytic gradient and boxes $|a| \le \nBoundA$, $|w_j|, |b| \le \nBoundW$.
The gradient is zero on clipped rows, where the loss is flat; a non-zero gradient there stops the optimiser after one step.

None of this is a new idea in general.
A ridge centred at a source fit is MAP adaptation \citep{chelba2006}, biased regularisation \citep{kuzborskij2013} and L2-SP \citep{li2018l2sp}; GLM transfer with a CV-chosen strength is studied by \citet{tian2023glmtrans}.
Our delta is applying it to the heteroscedastic probit with the three choices above, and measuring it against both endpoints.
Proposition~\ref{prop:slope} restates the classic local argument for this likelihood; the proof is in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.

\begin{proposition}[local risk, after \citealp{kuzborskij2013}]
\label{prop:slope}
Take the quadratic approximation of $\ell_T$ around the target optimum $\theta_*$, with total Fisher information $J \succ 0$ from $n$ target rows, penalty $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\theta-\theta_0)^\top \Lambda (\theta-\theta_0)$ with $\Lambda$ diagonal, $\Lambda \succeq 0$, $\Lambda \ne 0$, and gap $\delta = \theta_0 - \theta_*$.
The expected excess risk $R(\lambda)$ in the per-row metric $J/n$ has slope $R'(0) = -2\,\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda)/n < 0$.
So some $\lambda > 0$ beats target-only for any finite $\delta$, and for $\Lambda \succ 0$, $R(\lambda) \to \delta^\top (J/n)\, \delta$ as $\lambda \to \infty$.
\end{proposition}

The proposition says a small pull always helps in the quadratic regime.
It does not say cross-validation finds the right $\lambda$ on a few dozen groups, and the likelihood is not convex, so we claim no unique optimum.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.}
Football (play-by-play from \citealp{nflverse}): source seasons \nFbSrcYears{} (\nFbSrcGames{} games, \nFbSrcRows{} rows), target pool season \nFbPoolYears{} (\nFbFullGames{} games), test seasons \nFbTestYears{} (\nFbTestGames{} games, \nFbTestRows{} rows).
The distance is the home margin plus the pregame point spread times the share of the game left (Stern's drift); the \nFbNFeat{} scale features are the remaining time, the margin, timeouts left and the pregame total-points line.
Basketball \citep{sportsdataverse}: source men's league \nHpSrcYears{} (\nHpSrcGames{} games, \nHpSrcRows{} rows), target pool women's league \nHpPoolYears{} (\nHpFullGames{} games), test \nHpTestYears{} (\nHpTestGames{} games, \nHpTestRows{} rows), \nHpNFeat{} scale features (time, pace, margin).
Each game gives \nFbRowsPerGame{} random game states; $y = 1$ when the home team wins.
The test seasons come after every training season and nothing tunes on them.
A synthetic generator with known truth gives a regime map over \nSynCells{} cells (shift $\times$ target rows, \nSynSeeds{} draws each).

\textbf{Arms.}
Closed form ($\theta = 0$); target-only (plain variant, $\lambda$ by the same CV); source-only; pooled (source and target rows with a domain indicator in the scale); ours.
Generic learners: logistic regression \citep{pedregosa2011sklearn} target-only and pooled, LightGBM \citep{ke2017lightgbm} target-only, source-only and fine-tuned from the source booster.
\nAblations{} pre-registered ablations: $\lambda$ fixed at \nLamGridA, \nLamGridC{} and \nLamGridE, a Student-$t$(4) link, and target standardisation.
Every arm sees the same target rows; the generic learners pick their boosting rounds or penalty by the same grouped CV.

\textbf{Metric and SE.}
The primary metric is held-out log-loss $L = -\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i [y_i \log p_i + (1-y_i)\log(1-p_i)]$ in nats per row.
Target sizes are \nFbAGames, \nFbBGames, \nFbCGames, \nFbDGames{} (and \nHpEGames{} for basketball) games, \nSubsamples{} random subsamples each, plus the full pool.
The paired SE of $L_{\text{ours}} - L_{\text{arm}}$ is a block bootstrap over test games (\nBootDraws{} draws), resampling whole games because rows of one game are dependent, plus the between-subsample variance divided by \nSubsamples.
A difference inside $1.96$ SE is a tie.
Pre-registered: ours against target-only, source-only and pooled at every subsampled size, success if all three upper 95\% bounds are below zero on either real dataset.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}

The pre-registered success test failed on both real datasets.
The method reliably improves on the plain target fit, but on these tasks the best single choice was to use the source fit as it is.
After the main run we tried \nVariantsTried{} post-hoc variants of the $\lambda$ selection: a 13-point grid, slopes pulled far harder than the intercept, the one-standard-error rule and repeated CV (Appendix~\ref{app:variants}).
None was kept.
The one-SE rule picked $\lambda = \infty$ in every real subsample, so it is source-only by another name, and it loses on synthetic cells where the shift is real.
The \nAblations{} ablations were pre-registered.
We cut a third real dataset, the R baselines (no R install) and some ablations; Appendix~\ref{app:repro} lists them.
We do not claim the method beats source-only or pooling, nor that it generalises beyond these two sports and the synthetic generator.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:slope}}
\label{app:proof}

\textbf{Given.}
1) Quadratic approximation: $\ell_T(\theta) \approx \ell_T(\hat\theta) + \tfrac12 (\theta-\hat\theta)^\top J (\theta-\hat\theta)$ with $J \succ 0$ and $\hat\theta$ the unpenalised target fit.
2) $J(\hat\theta - \theta_*) = g$ with $\mathbb{E}[g] = 0$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(g) = J$.
3) Objective $Q_\lambda(\theta) = \ell_T(\theta) + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\theta-\theta_0)^\top\Lambda(\theta-\theta_0)$, $\Lambda$ diagonal, $\Lambda_{jj} \ge 0$, at least one $\Lambda_{jj} > 0$ (the package uses $\Lambda = I$).
4) Risk $R(\lambda) = \mathbb{E}\bigl[(\hat\theta(\lambda)-\theta_*)^\top (J/n) (\hat\theta(\lambda)-\theta_*)\bigr]$.
5) Matrix rules: $\tfrac{d}{d\lambda}A^{-1} = -A^{-1}\tfrac{dA}{d\lambda}A^{-1}$; $\mathrm{tr}(XY) = \mathrm{tr}(YX)$; $\mathbb{E}[g^\top M g] = \mathrm{tr}(MJ)$ for $\mathbb{E}[g] = 0$, $\mathrm{Cov}(g) = J$.

\textbf{Choose:} $A = J + \lambda\Lambda$ and $m = \lambda A^{-1}\Lambda\delta$.

\textbf{Need to prove:} $\hat\theta(\lambda) - \theta_* = m + A^{-1}g$.
\begin{align*}
0 &= J(\theta-\hat\theta) + \lambda\Lambda(\theta-\theta_0) && \text{gradient of $Q_\lambda$, given 1 and 3}\\
A\theta &= J\hat\theta + \lambda\Lambda\theta_0 && \text{collect the terms in $\theta$}\\
A(\theta-\theta_*) &= J(\hat\theta-\theta_*) + \lambda\Lambda(\theta_0-\theta_*) && \text{subtract $A\theta_* = J\theta_* + \lambda\Lambda\theta_*$}\\
\theta-\theta_* &= A^{-1}g + \lambda A^{-1}\Lambda\delta && \text{given 2, multiply by $A^{-1}$}
\end{align*}
The last line needs $A$ invertible, therefore, for $x \ne 0$:
\begin{align*}
x^\top A x &= x^\top J x + \lambda\, x^\top \Lambda x && \text{definition of $A$}\\
&> 0 && \text{$J \succ 0$, $\lambda \ge 0$, $\Lambda \succeq 0$}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Same method, on the risk.} Substitution in given 4:
\begin{align*}
R(\lambda) &= \mathbb{E}\bigl[(m + A^{-1}g)^\top \tfrac{J}{n} (m + A^{-1}g)\bigr] && \text{first part}\\
&= m^\top \tfrac{J}{n} m + 2\, m^\top \tfrac{J}{n} A^{-1}\mathbb{E}[g] + \mathbb{E}\bigl[g^\top A^{-1}\tfrac{J}{n}A^{-1} g\bigr] && \text{expand, $m$ is not random}\\
&= \underbrace{\lambda^2 \delta^\top\Lambda A^{-1}\tfrac{J}{n}A^{-1}\Lambda\delta}_{B(\lambda)} + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{n}\mathrm{tr}(A^{-1}JA^{-1}J)}_{V(\lambda)} && \text{$\mathbb{E}[g] = 0$, given 5}
\end{align*}

\textbf{Need to prove:} $R'(0) = -2\,\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda)/n$.
$B(\lambda) = \lambda^2\beta(\lambda)$ with $\beta$ smooth at $0$, so $B'(0) = 2\cdot 0\cdot\beta(0) + 0 = 0$. For the variance:
\begin{align*}
V'(\lambda) &= \tfrac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{tr}(-A^{-1}\Lambda A^{-1} J A^{-1} J) + \mathrm{tr}(A^{-1} J (-A^{-1}\Lambda A^{-1}) J)\bigr] && \text{product rule, given 5}\\
V'(0) &= -\tfrac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda J^{-1}J J^{-1}J) + \mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}J J^{-1}\Lambda J^{-1}J)\bigr] && \text{$A = J$ at $\lambda = 0$}\\
&= -\tfrac{1}{n}\bigl[\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) + \mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda J^{-1}J)\bigr] && \text{$J^{-1}J = I$}\\
&= -\tfrac{2}{n}\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) && \text{cyclic trace, given 5}
\end{align*}
The sign needs $\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) > 0$, therefore:
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda) &= \textstyle\sum_j (J^{-1})_{jj}\Lambda_{jj} && \text{$\Lambda$ diagonal}\\
&= \textstyle\sum_j (e_j^\top J^{-1} e_j)\,\Lambda_{jj} && \text{unit vectors $e_j$}\\
&> 0 && \text{$J^{-1} \succ 0$, all $\Lambda_{jj} \ge 0$, one $> 0$}
\end{align*}
\textbf{Therefore} $R'(0) = B'(0) + V'(0) = -2\,\mathrm{tr}(J^{-1}\Lambda)/n < 0$, and some $\lambda > 0$ has $R(\lambda) < R(0)$, the target-only risk.

\textbf{Same method, on the limit} ($\Lambda \succ 0$).
\begin{align*}
\lambda A^{-1} &= (J/\lambda + \Lambda)^{-1} \to \Lambda^{-1} && \text{divide $A$ by $\lambda$}\\
m &= \lambda A^{-1}\Lambda\delta \to \delta, \qquad A^{-1} \to 0 && \text{first line}\\
R(\lambda) &\to \delta^\top \tfrac{J}{n}\delta + 0 && \text{substitution in $B$ and $V$}
\end{align*}
That is the source-only risk. \qed

\section{Reproduction and cuts}
\label{app:repro}

The package is \texttt{scaleprobit} (Python, SciPy L-BFGS-B, \citealp{virtanen2020scipy}).
The benchmark runs on CPU in \nFbWallS{} s (football), \nHpWallS{} s (basketball) and \nSynWallS{} s (synthetic).
Commands, from the repository root: \texttt{python -m bench.run\_football}, \texttt{bench.run\_hoops}, \texttt{bench.run\_synthetic}, \texttt{bench.variants.run football|hoops}, \texttt{bench.variants.synthetic\_one\_se}; then \texttt{paper/results/refit\_full.py}, \texttt{paper/results/make\_numbers.py} and \texttt{paper/figures/make.py} regenerate every number and figure in this note.
The full-season refit reproduces the benchmark log-loss of every scaleprobit arm, and its per-game SE matches the block SE at the full season (\nFbFullSeGameTO{} against \nFbFullSeBlockTO{} on football).
Cuts, none silent: a third real dataset; the R arms \texttt{glmx::hetglm} \citep{zeileis_glmx} and \texttt{glmtrans}; the Platt, free-intercept and grouped-$\lambda$ ablations; the football source starts later than planned to bound download and fit time.
Basketball target files carry no usable pregame spread, so neither side uses Stern's drift there.
LightGBM uses one fixed parameter set; only its rounds are chosen by grouped CV.
